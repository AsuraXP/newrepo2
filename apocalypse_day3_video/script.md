Five fifty in the morning on Day Three. Shivering beside Maya behind granite boulders on the north pine ridge, you watch pale mist curl off the dark reservoir lake ahead. Below the trees rises the massive curved concrete wall of Clearwater Dam, gleaming untouched in grey dawn. To anyone exhausted after two days of running, that fenced dam looks like the safest fortress on the mountain. Before stepping onto the access road, you lift the brass binoculars from your coat and scan the entrance. Your pulse spikes. Down at the gatehouse, a jackknifed logging truck has smashed the chain link barrier open, and forty pale infected in yellow vests shamble across the lower turbine yard. Worse, crouching behind sandbags on the high steel catwalk sit two scavengers in dark red parkas beside stolen packs, watching a ration crate set dead center in the road as bait. Day One tested your reflexes, Day Two tested your wits, and Day Three reveals the deadliest threat left, desperate people waiting in ambush.

Walking down that open lane means walking straight into a trap, and turning south puts Twin Creek Gorge and yesterday's horde right in your path. Kneeling behind pine scrub, Maya unfolds the ranger map and taps a dotted line along the eastern cliff base, a concrete drainage tunnel cutting underneath the dam toward the upper reservoir dock. Staying out of sight below the catwalk, you and Maya creep down mossy maintenance steps tucked into the rock face and wedge your curved steel crowbar into a rusted iron service grate. One steady pull pops the corroded latch free without a sound. Inside, cold air smells of wet stone and machine oil. A bright flashlight beam could glint out through the bars, so Maya cracks a dim green chemical glow stick from her red trauma bag, casting just enough pale light to reveal ankle deep water and iron pipes lining a narrow concrete gallery.

Halfway through the dam's belly, the low tunnel meets a steel bulkhead door leading up to the boathouse stairs. Blocking the wheel latch stands a tall infected dam technician in rubber waders, swaying blindly toward water dripping from an overhead pipe. Swinging your crowbar inside a four foot passage leaves zero room to dodge, and one metallic strike against these pipes will echo straight up the vents to the men above. Glancing back, you spot a brass overflow wheel ten yards down the corridor beside a recessed floor culvert. Catching Maya's eye, you grip the cold wheel with both duct taped forearms and ease it counterclockwise. Water rushes loudly down the open culvert grate behind you. Drawn immediately by the splash, the pale technician lurches past your dark alcove toward the sound, letting you and Maya slip cleanly through the bulkhead door and ease the heavy steel latch shut behind you.

Narrow concrete steps bring you out behind a corrugated metal boathouse on the upper reservoir pier, shielded from the catwalk by three humming grey transformer boxes. Bobbing quietly against rubber dock bumpers sits your way out, an aluminum utility motorboat secured to a timber piling by an iron chain and brass padlock. Inside an open gear locker rest wooden oars and a full red five gallon gas can. Moving fast, Maya steps softly into the stern to clip the rubber fuel hose onto the outboard motor while you slide the hooked tip of your steel crowbar through the rusted padlock shackle. Before you can wrench it free, loose gravel crunches on the upper walkway forty yards above. One of the red parka scavengers leans over the steel railing, spots Maya's blue jacket on the pier, raises a black hunting rifle toward the roof, and yells down for you both to drop your packs and step back.

Dropping low behind a concrete bollard, you realize what the man on the railing overlooked, sheer canyon walls bounce every loud noise straight into the valley floor. Furious when neither of you surrenders your gear, the scavenger fires a warning shot that sparks off the tin boathouse roof. That sharp rifle crack slams off both cliffs like thunder and rolls into the lower yard. Below the dam, all forty pale infected freeze, snap their chalk white heads upward, and surge toward the open catwalk stairs. Rasping throats merge into a roar as dozens of figures in torn yellow vests scramble up the steel steps behind the ambush perch. Feeling the grating vibrate beneath their boots, the second scavenger spins around, shouts in panic, and both men bolt west across the dam crest toward an idling pickup as infected flood their sandbag nest.

That chaos buys you ten seconds at most, because six stray infected near the boat ramp heard the roof ricochet and sprint down the concrete slipway straight toward your pier. Throwing your full weight onto the steel crowbar, you snap the rusted brass padlock apart and whip the chain clear of the piling. In the stern, Maya pulls the starter cord once and gets a dry sputter, then squeezes the black fuel bulb firm and yanks hard a second time. The outboard catches with a throaty growl and white exhaust smoke. You plant one boot against the dock timber, shove the aluminum bow into deep water, and vault over the gunwale just as pale crooked hands swipe empty air above the pier edge. Opening the throttle, Maya steers a crisp white wake across calm mountain water toward a quiet pine covered island lookout half a mile north. With deep water guarding every side and a partner steady at the helm, you just survived Day Three.
