## Logline

You were bitten a week ago by a man who looked almost normal, went home, and slept. You wake up
ravenous to a phone that says seven days are gone — and the bite mark is gone too, replaced by skin
that closes itself. The city has a word for what is happening to people. It has no word for you,
because you never had stage one. You are not afraid of the bite. You are the bite.

## Genre frame (per user)

Not a "don't get bitten" survival story. The danger is not teeth. The danger is missing memory:
a body that keeps working while the person inside it is asleep, and a hunger that uses your own
hands. Horror of agency, not horror of chase.

## Director's critique of the supplied opening (what changed and why)

1. **The appetite is the clock, not the symptom.** In your version the hunger gets you out of bed
   before you look at your phone. Kept exactly that order — it is the whole story in miniature: the
   body acts first, the mind catches up later.
2. **The shove was the wrong kind of important.** Your version has you shove him *before* he lunges.
   Kept the shove, but framed it as ordinary self-defence with no guilt attached — so the audience
   files it away and forgets it, the way you did. Payoff arrives in Act IV, when the memory of it
   reorganizes into something you did not understand at the time.
3. **"He looked normal" was the load-bearing line.** Promoted to a direct address to yourself: no
   rot, no wound, nothing you could point at and call dangerous. That single decision is what makes
   the rest of the world plausible — nobody would have run.
4. **The week has to be provably *yours*.** Added evidence instead of exposition: fresh bin bag,
   double-knotted laces, keys in the sink, a cut chain re-hung. The apartment says you were home
   and you were busy; you have no access to it.
5. **Your own voice is the scariest artifact.** Added the nine-second voicemail you don't remember
   leaving. It sets up the Act III camera footage and the final "am I still in here" question.
6. **Cut a rescue.** No partner breaking down the door, no cure, no last-minute immunity. The video
   ends with you locking yourself in from the inside and talking, so the last image is a man
   choosing to be a person for as long as the door holds.
