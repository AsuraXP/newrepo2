# Apocalypse Survival — Script

## Episode 1: The First 72 Hours

You wake up and the world is different. The phones don't work. The roads are gridlocked with cars that ran out of gas. You can hear sirens in the distance but they're getting quieter, not closer. Whatever happened, it happened fast and it happened everywhere at once.

Here's the thing nobody tells you about the end of the world. It's not the monsters you need to worry about first. It's the clock.

Your body is a machine and machines run out of fuel. You have roughly seventy two hours before everything starts going wrong in ways you can't fix by running. So let's talk about what those three days actually look like.

Hour one. You're still in shock. Your brain is trying to process information that doesn't make sense yet. This is dangerous because shock makes you do stupid things like drive toward the city to see what's happening or stand outside staring at the sky. Do none of those things.

The first rule of apocalypse survival is the simplest and the hardest. Stop. Breathe. Think. Look around you right now and name three things. Where are you? What do you have? Who is with you? That inventory is your entire world now. Treat it like it matters because it does.

Hour two through twelve. Water. Not food. Water. Your body can survive weeks without eating but only days without drinking and in the chaos of a collapsing world dehydration will kill you faster than anything else. Find water now. Tap water might still work for a few hours but don't count on it. Fill every container you own. Bathtub, pots, bottles, plastic bags. Everything.

If the tap is already dead then you need to think about what's around you. Rainwater is your best friend if you can catch it. A river or stream works but you'll need to boil it. And here's a fact that sounds weird until you need it. The water heater in most homes holds thirty to eighty gallons of perfectly clean drinking water. Pop the valve at the bottom and you just solved your biggest problem.

Hours twelve through thirty six. Shelter and safety. You need a place where you can lock a door and control who comes in. Your own home might work if you live somewhere defensible. If not you need to move and you need to move smart.

Avoid the obvious places. Schools, hospitals, gas stations, grocery stores. Every panicked person who ever watched a movie is heading to those exact spots. You don't want to be there when they arrive empty handed and scared.

Instead look for the places people forget. The hardware store on the edge of town. The farm supply depot. The small church with a basement. These places have tools, they have supplies, and most importantly they have fewer people.

Hours thirty six through forty eight. Food becomes the priority now. Don't look for fancy meals. Look for calories. Canned beans, peanut butter, crackers, rice. Anything that lasts without refrigeration and doesn't need much preparation. You're not cooking. You're fueling.

But here's the part most survival guides skip. You need to think about security before you think about breakfast. A full pantry means nothing if someone can just walk in and take it. Check your doors. Check your windows. Figure out how many ways someone can enter your space and then figure out how to control each one.

Hours forty eight through seventy two. This is where the real test begins. The adrenaline is wearing off. You're tired, you're sore, and the reality of the situation is settling in like a weight on your chest. This is when people make their worst decisions. They give up. They panic. They try to contact family members who aren't answering and they waste precious hours watching a dead phone.

Don't do that. Accept the situation. Not forever. Just for right now. The person who survives the apocalypse is not the strongest or the smartest. It's the one who can sit in a quiet room and think clearly while the world falls apart outside.

So here's your checklist for the first three days. Water first. Then shelter. Then food. Then security. Then rest. Not in that exact order if circumstances force you to change it but that's the skeleton. Build everything else on top of it.

And one more thing. When you're ready, and only when you're ready, start thinking about other people. Lone wolves don't survive the long winter. Groups do. But you want to choose your group carefully and you want to be ready before you knock on anyone's door. Offer something. Skills, supplies, information. Never show up empty handed and scared.

The first seventy two hours are about staying alive. Everything after that is about deciding what kind of person you're going to be in the world that comes next. And that decision matters more than any supply run you'll ever make.

Stay calm. Stay ready. And remember that every single day you wake up breathing is a day you won.
