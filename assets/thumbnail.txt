DESCRIPTION
-----------
You wake up on a reed mat in a working town, five thousand years before you
were born. No alarm. No clock. No tap. Nothing in this world was built for you,
and almost everything in it can kill you slowly.

This is the honest version of the ancient-world daydream — not the heroic one.
We follow one day from first light to full dark: the brown canal water, the
bread with grit in it, the beer that's safer than the water, the teeth you wear
flat before forty, being paid in barley because coins don't exist yet, debt that
can reach your children, and a law system that runs on witnesses and payments
because there is no one to call.

Then the part nobody says out loud: what actually keeps you alive is being
useful, quiet, and patient. Not clever. Clever gets people accused of things.
