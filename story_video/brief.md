## Factual research

1. Ghibellini and Meier (2025), [Interruption, recall and resumption: a meta-analysis of the Zeigarnik and Ovsiankina effects](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w). The review found no consistent overall memory advantage for interrupted tasks; voluntary resumption across reviewed studies averaged roughly 67%. Do not equate recall and resumption or generalize the figure to all contexts.
2. Masicampo and Baumeister (2011), [Consider It Done! Plan Making Can Eliminate the Cognitive Effects of Unfulfilled Goals](https://users.wfu.edu/masicaej/MasicampoBaumeister2011JPSP.pdf). In their studies, specific plans reduced goal-related intrusions and interference; findings are context-dependent, not a guaranteed cure for worry.
3. Peper et al. (2023), [The role of offloading intentions on future-oriented thinking](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-023-02423-x). Reminders improved prospective-memory performance in the reported experiments, but did not reduce intention-related thoughts before the task. Distinguish reminder benefits from mental quiet.
