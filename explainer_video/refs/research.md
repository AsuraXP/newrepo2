# Research notes — script draft

Checked for the State 2 narration draft. Sources and qualifications live here rather than in the speakable `script.md`. Opening construction-site scenario and mismatched bolt are illustrative, not documented incidents.

- John Gardner and Ken Kiss, “Thread form at the Crystal Palace,” *The International Journal for the History of Engineering & Technology* (2024), https://doi.org/10.1080/17581206.2024.2391984 — Whitworth's 1841 proposal and 1905 formal adoption; surviving components examined; 25,000–30,000 estimated original nut-and-bolt pairs; researchers' conclusion about speed and standardized threads. The direct measurements include the rebuilt Sydenham site and adjacent water tower; do not extrapolate a small sample to every original fastener. The article explicitly treats the pace-of-construction conclusion as an argument, not controlled causal proof.
- Anglia Ruskin University, “ARU research solves mystery of the Crystal Palace,” https://www.aru.ac.uk/news/aru-research-solves-mystery-of-the-crystal-palace — dimensions, 3,300 columns, exhibition, Sydenham move and fire, the measured bolt and recreated bolt, and Whitworth's exhibition display. Its blanket assertion that no two earlier bolts were alike is not used; differing workshop practices are the more defensible claim.
- Bureau International des Expositions, “How the Crystal Palace was built in 190 days,” https://www.bie-paris.org/site/en/latest/blog/entry/how-the-crystal-palace-was-built-in-190-days — reported 190-day build, 560 m length and context. The script attributes the 190-day figure to a published account; construction timeline conventions can vary.
- Powerhouse Collection, “Lathe made by Joseph Whitworth,” https://collection.powerhouse.com.au/object/167800 — Whitworth's collection of workshop screws and 1841 proposal for thread angle and numbers of threads per inch.

(engineering overview), “Crystal Palace,” https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/crystal-palace — Paxton's glasshouse background, repetitive modular design, prefabrication, iron framing, and contributions from contractors.
